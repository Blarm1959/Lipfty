How to play

Lipfty

Objective. Lipfty is played on the inner 6×6 of a standard 8×8 chess/draughts board. The pieces are shared rather than owned by either player. Make one of the winning patterns allowed by the version you selected.

Two game formats. Lipfty is the full 32-piece game. Lipfty24 is the 24-piece version designed to match an ordinary draughts/checkers set exactly. Both formats offer Learning, Standard and Extreme.

Full Lipfty setup. Four special pieces begin automatically on the four corners of the inner 6×6 as the Opening Four, with matching colours diagonally opposite. The outer ring holds 24 ordinary reserve pieces plus four special corner pieces which later become the Final Four. The Opening Four are pinned: they count towards wins but cannot Move or Jump and cannot be jumped over.

Playing full Lipfty with a combined chess/draughts set. Use the 24 draughts/checkers men as the normal reserve and use the eight chess pawns as Lipfty's special pieces: four for the Opening Four and four for the Final Four. The app shows these eight special pieces with a pawn shape to mirror the physical game.

Lipfty24 setup. Use exactly 24 ordinary draughts/checkers pieces. Put them around the outside edge in every non-corner position, leaving the four physical board corners empty. The inner 6×6 starts empty. Lipfty24 has no Opening Four and no Final Four; every piece is an ordinary shared piece.

Versions. Learning uses horizontal, vertical and diagonal four-in-line wins and is placement-only. Standard uses the same line wins with Place, Move and Jump. Extreme uses Standard rules and also allows a tight 2×2 Square and a spaced board-aligned Square.

First turn and normal handover. The starting player is selected according to the game setup. The opponent chooses the first ordinary reserve piece and hands it to the starter. Thereafter, the player who completes a normal turn chooses an actual remaining reserve piece and hands it to the opponent. Its colour is the colour the receiver must use.

Place. In Learning, place the handed reserve piece on any empty square. In Standard and Extreme, placement is also always available.

Move (Standard and Extreme). Instead of placing the handed piece, move one movable board piece of that colour exactly one empty adjacent square horizontally, vertically or diagonally. In full Lipfty, Opening Four pieces are pinned and cannot Move. You may not Move or Jump the exact piece your opponent moved on their immediately preceding turn.

Move consequence. Keep the reserve piece you were handed. Choose a different available reserve piece for your opponent to place. If that does not win, place the piece you were originally handed. The opponent then takes the next normal turn.

Jump (Standard and Extreme). Leap one movable board piece of the handed colour over one adjacent opposite-colour movable piece into the empty square immediately beyond, horizontally, vertically or diagonally. There are no multi-jumps. In full Lipfty, a pinned Opening Four piece cannot be jumped over. A winning Jump ends the game immediately.

Jump consequence. Keep the reserve piece you were handed. Lift the exact jumped piece and give it to your opponent to redeploy on any empty square. If that does not win, place the piece you were originally handed. The opponent then takes the next normal turn.

One-colour finish. As soon as either ordinary reserve colour is exhausted, Move and Jump stop. Place the remaining ordinary reserve pieces one per normal turn until the ordinary reserve is empty. Finish any Move consequence already under way first.

Ending full Lipfty. When the 24 ordinary reserve pieces are gone, the four special outer-corner pieces become the Final Four. They are placed one per turn. If all four are placed without a win, the game is a draw.

Ending Lipfty24. There is no Final Four. If all 24 ordinary pieces have been used without a win, the game is a draw.

Immediate wins. A win ends the game immediately whether it is made by a normal placement, Move, Jump, Jump redeployment, held-piece placement or, in full Lipfty, Final Four placement.

Undo and chess clock. The optional chess-style clock can give each player 5, 10, 15, 30 or 60 minutes, with an optional 5- or 10-second increment. Only the player currently making the decision has their clock running. Running out of time loses the game. Undo restores the clocks with the restored decision.
