Turn choices

First four turns: alternate normally. On your turn choose and place any remaining top-corner piece. No colour is given; moving and jumping are not allowed.

Then: use the colour your opponent gives you. Place that colour or move a board piece of that colour. Jumping unlocks permanently once only eight of the 28 normal playable pieces remain in the outer reserve.

If you move, take any two corner markers off the board. Each of the next two compulsory reserve placements returns one marker.

If you jump, you may make at most two jumps. Each jumped piece goes on top of a corner marker, and two other single corner markers come off the board to show that the next two turns are compulsory placements. Jumped corner pieces are placed first and count towards those two placements.

Final four: when all 28 normal playable pieces are on the board, moving and jumping end. Players alternately choose and place the four corner-marker pieces. A win ends the game immediately; if all four are placed without a win, the game is a draw.

You cannot move the piece your opponent moved on their previous turn.

How to play

Lipfty 4

Objective. Lipfty is played on the inner 6×6 area of a standard 8×8 board using a full 32-piece draughts/checkers set. Fourteen pieces of each colour are playable; one extra piece of each colour starts underneath each of that colour's two corner reserve pieces. The playable pieces are shared by both players. Online they are shown as Red and Blue. The four underneath corner pieces act as physical markers during normal play and become the final four playable pieces if the game reaches that point. Win with four pieces of the same colour either consecutive in a horizontal, vertical or diagonal straight line, or on the four corners of any square aligned at 90° or 45° to the board.

Opening play. Choose the first player, then alternate normally. For the first four turns, the player whose turn it is chooses one of the four playable top-corner pieces and places it on any empty square in the inner 6×6 area. These four turns are placement-only: the opponent does not choose the colour and no piece may be moved or jumped. Once all four top-corner pieces have been placed, normal colour-giving begins: the player who has just completed a turn chooses which colour the opponent must use.

Normal play. Use the colour your opponent gives you. You may place a reserve piece of that colour or move an existing board piece of that colour one adjacent square. Once only eight normal playable pieces remain in the outer reserve, jumping unlocks permanently for the rest of the main game. Moves and jumps may be horizontal, vertical or diagonal.

Move compulsory placements. If you make an ordinary one-square move instead of placing or jumping, temporarily remove any two of the four bottom corner-marker pieces; their colours do not matter. The next two turns must be reserve placements, first by your opponent and then by you. After each placement return one marker to an empty outer corner. If no normal reserve piece is available, any remaining placement obligation ends and normal play resumes.

Jumping. A jump goes over an adjacent piece into the empty square immediately beyond it. A multi-jump may contain at most two jumps. Every piece jumped over is removed from the 6×6 board and placed on top of a corner marker. After any jump turn, remove two of the remaining single corner markers from the board: the next two turns are compulsory placements.

During one multi-jump you may not return to a square already visited. If Undo Previous Jump is enabled in Settings, an immediate jump back to the square just left is allowed, but longer loops are still not allowed. The jump ends after the second jump or when no legal continuation is available.

After a jump. If a jumped piece is sitting on top of a corner marker, it has priority during the two compulsory placement turns. The player chooses which available top-corner piece to place; the opponent does not give its colour. Each such return counts as one of the two compulsory placements. If only one piece was jumped, the second compulsory turn is a normal reserve placement. No moving or jumping is allowed until both compulsory placement turns are complete.

You may not move the exact piece your opponent moved on their immediately preceding turn. A newly placed piece is not protected by this rule.

Final four and draw. When all 28 normal playable pieces are simultaneously on the 6×6 board, normal play ends permanently. The four corner-marker pieces now become playable. Players alternate; on each turn the player chooses any remaining corner-marker piece and places it on an empty square. There is no moving or jumping. A win takes effect immediately. If all four final pieces are placed without a win, the game is a draw. A genuine earlier stalemate is also a draw.

Winning. Four consecutive matching-colour pieces in any horizontal, vertical or diagonal straight line wins, as do matching pieces on the four corners of any board-aligned or 45° diamond square. A win takes effect immediately, including during a multi-jump or compulsory placement.

Undo and timer. If last-move Undo is enabled, the previous play can be undone even after a win, including a computer win. The move timer may be set to 30, 45 or 60 seconds, or Unlimited. Reaching zero does not force the turn to end. The timer stops when the game is won or drawn and resumes appropriately if a winning play is undone.
