Lipfty 8 Standard

Opening Four: four pieces start automatically on the four corners of the inner 6×6, with matching colours diagonally opposite. They remain pinned for the whole game: they cannot Move, Jump or be jumped over, but they count normally towards a win.

Main play: the opponent of the starting player hands over the first normal reserve piece. Use its colour to Place it, Move a matching movable board piece one adjacent square, or make one Jump over an adjacent opposite-colour movable piece.

After a Move: the responder chooses and makes one compulsory reserve placement, then chooses a second reserve piece for the original mover to place. The responder then takes the next normal turn.

After a Jump: if the Jump itself does not win, lift the jumped piece. The opponent redeploys that exact piece anywhere empty and then takes the next normal turn; the original jumper chooses the reserve colour for that turn.

One-colour finish: as soon as either normal reserve colour is exhausted, Move and Jump stop. Every remaining normal reserve piece must then be placed.

Final Four: after the normal reserve is empty, players choose and place the four corner pieces. Final Four is placement-only.

Wins: four horizontal, vertical or diagonal; a tight 2×2 square; or a spaced board-aligned square. A win ends the game immediately.

How to play

Lipfty 8

Objective. Lipfty uses a standard 8×8 draughts/checkers board and full 32-piece set. Play takes place on the inner 6×6 area. The pieces are shared by both players rather than owned by one player. Win by making four matching-colour pieces horizontally, vertically or diagonally, or by making the four corners of a tight 2×2 square or a larger board-aligned spaced square.

Piece groups. Four pieces begin automatically on the four corners of the inner 6×6 as the Opening Four anchors. The outer ring holds 24 normal reserve pieces (12 of each colour), plus four corner pieces which become the Final Four.

Opening Four. Before either player starts, the four opening pieces are placed automatically on the four corners of the inner 6×6. Matching colours are diagonally opposite. These four pieces are permanent anchors: they cannot Move or Jump, and no Jump may pass over them. They still count normally in every winning pattern.

First turn and normal handover. After the anchors are in place, the starting player is selected according to the game setup. The opponent chooses the first normal reserve piece and hands it to the starting player. After that, the player who has just completed a normal turn chooses an actual normal reserve piece and hands it to the opponent. Its colour is the colour the receiving player must use. While both normal reserve colours remain, the receiver may either place that handed piece, Move a matching movable board piece, or make one legal Jump with a matching movable board piece.

Move. Move one movable board piece of the handed colour exactly one square to any empty adjacent square: horizontal, vertical or diagonal. The four opening anchors can never Move or Jump. You may not Move or Jump the exact board piece your opponent moved on their immediately preceding turn.

Move consequence. A non-winning Move does not consume the handed reserve piece. Instead, the opponent chooses a normal reserve piece and immediately places it. If that does not win, the same opponent chooses another remaining normal reserve piece for the original mover, who places it. If neither placement wins, the opponent takes the next normal turn.

Jump. Make one non-capturing-style leap with a movable board piece of the handed colour over one adjacent opposite-colour movable piece into the empty square immediately beyond it. The four pinned opening anchors cannot be jumped over. Jumps may be horizontal, vertical or diagonal and there are no multi-jumps. If the Jump itself makes a win, the game ends before anything else happens.

Jump consequence. If the Jump does not win, lift the exact jumped piece. The opponent immediately redeploys that same piece on any empty square. A win made by the redeployment ends the game. Otherwise the opponent gets the next normal turn, and the original jumper chooses the normal reserve piece/colour handed to them. A Jump consumes no normal reserve piece.

One-colour finish. As soon as either normal reserve colour is exhausted, Move and Jump stop completely. The remaining normal reserve pieces must be placed, one per normal turn, until the normal reserve is empty. If a two-placement Move consequence has already started, finish that consequence first.

Final Four. When the normal reserve is empty, the four corner pieces become playable. Final Four is placement-only. On each turn the player chooses one of the remaining Final Four pieces and places it anywhere empty. If all four are placed without a win, the game is a draw.

Immediate wins. A win ends the game immediately whether it is made by a normal placement, Move, Jump, Jump redeployment, compulsory placement or Final Four placement.

Standard and variants. Lipfty 8 Standard uses the automatic pinned diagonal-corner opening, with Move, Jump, Diagonal, Tight Square and Spaced Square switched on; Diamonds are off. The setup Rules page keeps switches available for experimental or simpler variants, but the Lipfty 8 Standard baseline is the configuration above.

Undo and timer. If Undo is enabled, the previous decision can be restored even after a win. The timer may be 30, 45 or 60 seconds, or Unlimited; reaching zero does not force a turn to end.
