Inner 6×6 = playing area · outer ring = 14 playable Red + 14 playable Blue reserve pieces. Each corner starts as a same-colour stack of two. Play the four top corner pieces first; the four underneath pieces then act as corner markers.

Turn choices

First four turns: alternate normally. On your turn choose and place any remaining top-corner piece. No colour is given; moving and jumping are not allowed.

Then: use the colour your opponent gives you. Place that colour, move one adjacent square, or make a single jump over an adjacent piece of the opposite colour into the empty square beyond it. The jumped piece stays on the board.

If you move or jump, take any two corner markers off the board. Each of the next two compulsory reserve placements returns one marker.

Final four: when all 28 normal playable pieces are on the board, moving and jumping end. Players alternately choose and place the four corner-marker pieces. A win ends the game immediately; if all four are placed without a win, the game is a draw.

You cannot move the piece your opponent moved on their previous turn.

How to play

Lipfty 4

Objective. Lipfty is played on the inner 6×6 area of a standard 8×8 board using a full 32-piece draughts/checkers set. Fourteen pieces of each colour are playable; one extra piece of each colour starts underneath each of that colour's two corner reserve pieces. The playable pieces are shared by both players. Online they are shown as Red and Blue. The four underneath corner pieces act as physical markers during normal play and become the final four playable pieces if the game reaches that point. Win with four pieces of the same colour either consecutive in a horizontal, vertical or diagonal straight line, or on the four corners of a board-aligned square. By default only a tight 2×2 square counts; the Allow spaced corners option also permits larger board-aligned squares.

Opening play. Choose the first player, then alternate normally. For the first four turns, the player whose turn it is chooses one of the four playable top-corner pieces and places it on any empty square in the inner 6×6 area. These four turns are placement-only: the opponent does not choose the colour and no piece may be moved or jumped. Once all four top-corner pieces have been placed, normal colour-giving begins: the player who has just completed a turn chooses which colour the opponent must use.

Normal play. Use the colour your opponent gives you. You may place a reserve piece of that colour, move an existing board piece of that colour one adjacent square, or make a single jump with that colour. Moves and jumps may be horizontal, vertical or diagonal.

Moving and jumping. A jump goes over one adjacent piece of the opposite colour into the empty square immediately beyond it. The jumped-over piece stays on the board. Only one jump is allowed in a turn; there are no multi-jumps.

Compulsory placements. An ordinary move or a jump has the same consequence: temporarily remove any two of the four bottom corner-marker pieces; their colours do not matter. The next two turns must be reserve placements, first by the opponent and then by the player who moved or jumped. After each placement return one marker to an empty outer corner. No moving or jumping is allowed during these compulsory placement turns. If no normal reserve piece is available, any remaining placement obligation ends.

You may not move the exact piece your opponent moved on their immediately preceding turn. A newly placed piece is not protected by this rule.

Final four and draw. When all 28 normal playable pieces are simultaneously on the 6×6 board, normal play ends permanently. The four corner-marker pieces now become playable. Players alternate; on each turn the player chooses any remaining corner-marker piece and places it on an empty square. There is no moving or jumping. A win takes effect immediately. If all four final pieces are placed without a win, the game is a draw. A genuine earlier stalemate is also a draw.

Winning. Four consecutive matching-colour pieces in any horizontal, vertical or diagonal straight line wins, as do matching pieces on the four corners of a board-aligned square. By default the square must be a tight 2×2; enable Allow spaced corners to permit larger squares. A win takes effect immediately, including after a jump or compulsory placement.

Undo and timer. If last-move Undo is enabled, the previous play can be undone even after a win, including a computer win. The move timer may be set to 30, 45 or 60 seconds, or Unlimited. Reaching zero does not force the turn to end. The timer stops when the game is won or drawn and resumes appropriately if a winning play is undone.
